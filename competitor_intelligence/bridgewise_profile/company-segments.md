# ICP — company segments

**Owner:** Shany Vinokur · **review_by:** 2026-11-26 · **sources:** `Bridgewise Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)` (Shany Vinokur, routed 2026-08-26) · external figures cited inline · `references/review-cadences.md` (ICP = quarterly)

Ten segments, in priority order as ranked in [`README.md`](README.md#where-to-focus). Product, offering grid and the approved proof chain live in [`README.md`](README.md) — read it first. Personas per segment are in [`personas.md`](personas.md).

BridgeWise's ICP is unusual because one embedded investment AI serves the entire financial services value chain: from the exchanges where securities are listed, through the data providers who distribute information, to the brokerages and banks where end investors trade.

---

## 1. Online trading platforms and retail brokerages

Our original beachhead and still the highest-volume segment. Trading platforms compete on features, and the quality of the investment experience correlates directly with trading frequency and retention. Most platforms provide technical analysis tools and newsfeeds but leave fundamental analysis to the investor, or cover only the top few hundred large-caps.

BridgeWise closes that gap with analysis on 60,000 assets, white-labelled to look native, plus the proactive layer that reaches investors between logins. The commercial argument is the full chain (see [the approved proof chain](README.md#the-approved-proof-chain)). The eToro partnership launched AI-guided mid-cap portfolios. IBI Investment House (Israel's second-largest brokerage) reports the BridgeWise section is one of the most viewed on their platform.

**Example targets:** eToro, Interactive Brokers, Robinhood, Webull, TradeStation, Trading212, Saxo Bank, tastytrade, Fidelity's brokerage division, Schwab retail.

**Why they buy:** Differentiation in a commoditised market · engagement and retention · coverage of long-tail assets · multilingual support for international expansion · MiFID II compliance for European operations · a faster path than building in-house.

**Offering:** Bridgewise for Retail Brokerages, with Frontier where the platform wants to offer managed portfolios without building a wealth arm.

## 2. CFD and forex platforms

A distinct sub-segment we target with dedicated one-pagers, a Bridget for CFD product and a CFD-specific brochure. CFD platforms serve active traders who make high-frequency decisions and depend on timely, actionable intelligence. The **FXBO CRM integration** (announced April 2026) embeds BridgeWise into forex broker CRM workflows, and the **Devexperts partnership** connects Bridget to the DXtrade platform used by dozens of CFD brokers.

**Example targets:** Plus500 (published case study), XM (published case study), AvaTrade (published case study), IG Group, CMC Markets, Pepperstone, OANDA, FXCM, Capital.com, easyMarkets.

**Why they buy:** Active traders demand more to act on · Always-on Awareness increases trade frequency without adding campaign load · regulatory requirements for providing research are tightening · Bridget adds a premium experience competitors cannot ship quickly.

**Offering:** Bridgewise for Retail Brokerages.

## 3. Stock exchanges

A **uniquely strategic segment** where we have no direct competitor at scale. Exchanges want to attract investors to their listed companies, improve issuer visibility (especially for small and mid-caps that lack analyst coverage), and monetise their data. BridgeWise fills the coverage gap: **only ~5,000 of 50,000+ global stocks receive regular analyst coverage**, leaving the vast majority invisible to investors.

Japan Exchange Group (JPX) launched "JPX Market Explorer" powered by BridgeWise, covering **all ~3,900 TSE-listed stocks**. B3 (Brazilian Stock Exchange) is both a customer and an investor. SIX Swiss Exchange is a lead investor and distributes BridgeWise across European broker terminals. The Tel Aviv Stock Exchange and London Stock Exchange are also partners.

**Example targets:** Nasdaq, NYSE/ICE, Deutsche Börse, Euronext, Hong Kong Exchanges (HKEX), Singapore Exchange (SGX), Korea Exchange (KRX), ASX, Bursa Malaysia, BSE/NSE (India), Tadawul.

**Why they buy:** Visibility and liquidity for listed issuers · attract retail investors with analysis reaching the whole listed universe · revenue from data monetisation · differentiation from competing exchanges · multilingual access for international investors.

**Offering:** Bridgewise for Retail Brokerages, distributed to member brokers.

## 4. Traditional banks with investment and advisory services

Banks with retail and private banking divisions need to serve millions of clients with investment guidance, but their human analyst teams cover only a fraction of the market. Israel Discount Bank, Bank Hapoalim, Leumi Bank, Standard Bank, Emirates NBD, KBank (Thailand) and Yahav Bank are all clients.

The **advisor retirement crisis** makes AI augmentation existential here. McKinsey projects **110,000 US financial advisors (38% of the workforce) will retire by 2034**. The **Great Wealth Transfer** of $84–105T to digital-native heirs compounds the urgency.

Banks straddle both segments. The retail brokerage arm buys the retail offering and the retail proof chain applies. The advisory arm buys the advisory offering, where **no approved metrics exist** and the pitch runs on capability and compliance instead.

**Example targets:** JPMorgan Chase, UBS, HSBC, Citi, Barclays, BNP Paribas, Deutsche Bank, Standard Chartered, DBS Group, National Australia Bank, Itaú Unibanco, Santander.

**Why they buy:** Expand coverage without adding headcount · raise advisor productivity · compete with fintechs for younger clients · support a multilingual client base · meet regulatory requirements for AI-generated content · demonstrate digital innovation.

**Offering:** Both, usually sequenced. Land in one division and expand.

## 5. Wealth management and advisory firms

The global wealth management market generates **$1.6T in annual revenue** and manages **$90.5T in US AUM alone**. There are **15,870+ SEC-registered investment advisers** in the US, the vast majority small firms (87.7% have fewer than 50 employees) that cannot afford proprietary research teams.

The problem this segment already describes to itself: growth is trapped between scale and quality. More clients means more advisors and higher cost, or the same team stretched thinner and quality splitting. Actionable Intelligence means no advisor starts from a blank page and every advisor in the firm works from the same analysis. **Always-on Awareness is the capability that sells this segment**, because it answers the question that governs an advisor's day: out of two hundred clients, who needs me today, and why. Bridget removes the "let me look into that and come back to you." Frontier hands the advisor a ready-to-approve move, which unlocks the accounts that were never worth taking on and the AUM sitting idle inside the existing book.

**73% of wealth managers already use AI** for investment operations, and 9 out of 10 believe AI can help grow their book by 20%+.

**Example targets:** Charles Schwab (advisory division), Fidelity, Edward Jones, Raymond James, LPL Financial, Stifel, Ameriprise, RBC Wealth Management, RIA aggregators (Dynasty Financial, Focus Financial), and the thousands of independent RIAs seeking competitive tools.

**Why they buy:** Raise the client-to-advisor ratio without lowering quality · cover the whole book rather than the top of it · cut time to review a portfolio · retain clients through the Great Wealth Transfer · comply with SEC Reg BI and MiFID II.

**What they measure:** clients per advisor, retention and AUM growth as hard metrics; time to review a portfolio and percentage of book actively covered as soft ones. **Clients per advisor is the headline**, because a COO can model it in their head while you are still talking.

**Offering:** Bridgewise for Wealth Advisory, with Frontier as the growth argument.

## 6. Neobanks and digital banks

The fastest-growing segment (**45–49% CAGR**) with **350M+ projected users by 2026**, but **~80% remain unprofitable**. Adding investment services with embedded AI creates premium subscription tiers, increases stickiness, and opens commission and trading revenue, all without building analytical capabilities in-house. Revolut's expansion into brokerage and SoFi's evolution into a full financial platform illustrate the trend.

**Example targets:** Revolut, N26, Chime, SoFi, Nubank, Monzo, Starling Bank, Wise, Bunq, Cash App (Block), Varo Bank, Current.

**Why they buy:** Revenue diversification beyond interchange · stickiness via investment features · speed to market through white-label APIs · multilingual support for cross-border operations · differentiation against both traditional banks and other neobanks.

**Offering:** Bridgewise for Retail Brokerages. Frontier is a strong fit — a neobank can offer managed investing without building a wealth business.

## 7. Robo-advisors

Robo-advisors manage **$1.97T in AUM** and are growing at **24–33% CAGR**, but face a transparency deficit: clients invest in algorithmic portfolios without understanding the underlying holdings. Actionable Intelligence addresses this directly by breaking funds and ETFs into constituent assets with analysis attached. The hybrid model, combining digital and human advice, is the fastest-growing sub-segment.

**Example targets:** Betterment, Wealthfront (now Schwab), Schwab Intelligent Portfolios, Nutmeg (JPMorgan), Scalable Capital, Moneyfarm, Ellevest, Wealthsimple.

**Why they buy:** Transparency on underlying holdings · differentiation from commodity robo competitors · support for hybrid advisory models · engagement beyond quarterly rebalancing · analysis pure algorithmic robos cannot provide.

**Offering:** Bridgewise for Retail Brokerages, or Wealth Advisory where a human advisor sits in the loop.

## 8. Asset managers, hedge funds and buy-side quants

The strongest growth segment post-acquisition, and the one where the asset mix changed most. Over **95% of hedge fund managers now use generative AI** (AIMA 2025 survey), and the AI-in-asset-management market is projected to reach **$17–47B by 2030**. Even large funds can only deeply analyse a fraction of the investable universe — BlackRock processes **5,000+ earnings call transcripts per quarter** and **6,000+ broker reports daily**.

Two distinct buyer motions live here.

**Fundamental and discretionary managers** buy Actionable Intelligence for coverage breadth: screening thousands of names, covering markets where language barriers limit human analysis, and supplementing thin analyst coverage.

**Quantitative and systematic funds** buy alternative data. The Context Analytics acquisition brings sentiment signals derived from news, social media and filings, delivered as a raw feed rather than a client-facing surface. This audience evaluates on signal quality, history depth, point-in-time integrity and backtest performance, not on UX. S&P Global Market Intelligence is already a Context Analytics customer, and the Eagle Alpha conference circuit is the primary channel for reaching this buyer directly.

**Example targets:** BlackRock, Vanguard, State Street, T. Rowe Price, Wellington Management, Point72, Citadel, Millennium Management, Man Group, Schroders, Two Sigma, AQR, Balyasny, and the long tail of mid-size and emerging managers.

**Why they buy:** Screen thousands of securities at scale · cover markets where language barriers limit human analysis · reduce time from data to insight · supplement analyst coverage · source differentiated alternative data signals with verifiable history.

**How they buy:** Not a standard SaaS evaluation. Expect a trial data cut, an internal backtest and a quant team verdict before any commercial conversation. Cycles are shorter than bank cycles but gate entirely on signal performance. **None of the retail proof chain applies.**

**Offering:** Sits outside the four-offering grid. Sold as a data relationship.

## 9. Financial data providers and market data companies

Data providers are under pressure from AI-native disruptors and must add analytical capabilities to retain clients. **FactSet already distributes BridgeWise analysis** through its marketplace, validating the integration model. The global financial data market is **$28–31B**, growing at **8.6% CAGR**. Providers can offer BridgeWise as a premium add-on, enriching raw data with actionable analysis.

**Example targets:** Bloomberg, Refinitiv (LSEG), S&P Global, FactSet, Morningstar, ICE Data Services, Nasdaq Data, Dun & Bradstreet, Moody's Analytics.

**Why they buy:** Enrich data feeds with AI-generated analysis · defend market share against AI-native competitors · create new premium tiers · extend coverage to securities lacking human analyst reports.

**Offering:** Distribution partnership rather than a segment offering.

## 10. Insurance investment arms and pension funds

A lower-priority, exploratory segment. Large but slow, and **we have no reference customer here yet**. The top 20 insurers collectively hold **$10T+ in assets**, manage diversified equity portfolios, and face Solvency II requirements demanding detailed investment risk monitoring.

The plausible entry point is portfolio monitoring at scale plus audit-ready documentation, not investor engagement — which means most of the capability story and all of the proof chain do not apply. **Treat this as opportunistic inbound rather than an outbound target list until a first logo lands.**

**Example targets:** Allianz (PIMCO/AGI), AXA, Prudential, MetLife, Manulife, Zurich Insurance, Generali, Aviva, and large pension funds (CalPERS, OTPP, Government Pension Fund of Norway).

**Why they buy:** Portfolio monitoring at scale · regulatory compliance documentation · ESG screening · policyholder communication · cost efficiency against maintaining large in-house research teams.
