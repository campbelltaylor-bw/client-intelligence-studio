# ICP — how the sales cycle works at financial institutions

**Owner:** Shany Vinokur · **review_by:** 2026-11-26 · **sources:** `Bridgewise Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)` (Shany Vinokur, routed 2026-08-26) · `references/review-cadences.md` (ICP = quarterly)

The shape of a BridgeWise deal: how long, who is in the room, and what actually moves it. Who those people are is in [`personas.md`](personas.md); which segment they sit in is in [`company-segments.md`](company-segments.md).

## Length, by institution size

Selling to financial institutions is a **multi-stakeholder, 3–18 month cycle** that varies by institution size.

| Institution | Typical cycle |
| --- | --- |
| Mid-size fintechs | **3–6 months** |
| Mid-size banks | **6–12 months** |
| Tier-1 banks | **9–18+ months** |
| Buy-side quants | Shorter, but gates on backtest performance rather than committee process |

## The standard process

Problem identification → initial research → RFI/RFP → proof of value or pilot → security and compliance review → legal negotiation → budget approval → deployment.

Buy-side quant deals are the exception and do not follow this at all — expect a trial data cut, an internal backtest and a quant team verdict before any commercial conversation (see [segment 8](company-segments.md#8-asset-managers-hedge-funds-and-buy-side-quants)).

## What moves a deal

**Land small, then expand.** Banks expect a **5–10x ROI multiple** on value delivered versus cost. The most effective pattern is to land a smaller, high-impact pilot and then expand across the institution. **Discount Bank, JPX and B3 all followed this approach.**

**Multi-thread from the start.** Engaging the champion (CPO / Head of Trading), the economic buyer (CEO / CIO), the technical buyer (CTO) and the compliance gatekeeper **at the same time** increases win rates substantially over single-threaded selling.

**Surface the missing names.** The buying committee averages **five to six people** and is rarely all present at the second meeting. Finding out who is not in the room is part of the close.

**Engage Compliance early, deliberately.** Compliance review is the most common cause of a stall and can take months. Providing compliance documentation before it is asked for is what keeps this stakeholder from becoming a blocker — the architecture argument is in [`personas.md`](personas.md#head-of-compliance--the-gatekeeper-who-can-kill-deals).

## Which numbers travel in which room

Per [the approved proof chain](README.md#the-approved-proof-chain):

- **Retail conversations** — lead with the chain, in order: 10% of alerts clicked → 1 in 8 engagements becomes a trade → 1.8x revenue from investors with access versus those without.
- **Advisory conversations** — lead with clients per advisor and percentage of book covered. **Carry no performance numbers** until an advisory proof point is signed off.
- **Quant buyers** — lead with signal history, point-in-time integrity and backtest results.
